Learn about the inclination of a line and the equation of a straight line.

Unit 1: The Inclination of a Line

1. What is the inclination of a line?

The inclination of a straight line is the angle measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis to the straight line.

The angle of inclination is represented by the Greek letter θ (theta).

Important:

The inclination is measured between 0° and 180°.

2. Understanding different inclinations

  • 0°: Horizontal line.
  • Between 0° and 90°: Line slopes upwards from left to right.
  • 90°: Vertical line.
  • Between 90° and 180°: Line slopes downwards from left to right.

3. Relationship between gradient and inclination

The gradient of a straight line is related to its inclination using the tangent ratio.

m = tan θ

Where:

  • m = gradient
  • θ = angle of inclination

4. Important gradient facts

If 0° < θ < 90°, the gradient is positive.

If 90° < θ < 180°, the gradient is negative.

At 0°, the gradient is 0.

At 90°, the gradient is undefined.

5. Finding inclination from gradient

When the gradient is given, use the inverse tangent function to find the inclination.

θ = tan⁻¹(m)

Remember that the inclination must be between 0° and 180°.

If the gradient is negative, the inclination is between 90° and 180°.

Unit 2: The Equation of a Straight Line

1. What is the equation of a straight line?

The equation of a straight line describes the relationship between the x-coordinate and y-coordinate of points on a straight line.

2. General equation of a straight line

y = mx + c

Where:

  • y = y-coordinate
  • x = x-coordinate
  • m = gradient
  • c = y-intercept

3. The gradient

The gradient measures the steepness and direction of a straight line.

If two points are given, the gradient can be calculated using:

m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)

4. The y-intercept

The y-intercept is the point where the straight line crosses the y-axis.

At the y-intercept, x = 0.

5. Point-gradient form

When the gradient and one point on the line are given, use the following formula:

y - y₁ = m(x - x₁)

6. Finding the equation using two points

When two points are given:

  1. Calculate the gradient.
  2. Choose either of the two points.
  3. Substitute into the point-gradient formula.
  4. Simplify to obtain y = mx + c.
Remember:

Parallel straight lines have equal gradients.

A horizontal line has a gradient of zero.

A vertical line has an undefined gradient and cannot be written in the form y = mx + c.
