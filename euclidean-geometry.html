Euclidean Geometry

Euclidean Geometry studies points, lines, angles and shapes. In this topic, we will focus on circle geometry and learn how to use geometric theorems to solve problems.

Unit 1: Circles

1. Parts of a Circle

A circle is a closed curved shape in which every point on the circumference is the same distance from the centre.

Diagram 1: Parts of a Circle

A diameter passes through the centre. A radius joins the centre to the circumference. A chord joins two points on the circumference.

  • Centre: The middle point of a circle.
  • Radius: A line from the centre to the circumference.
  • Diameter: A chord passing through the centre.
  • Chord: A line joining two points on the circumference.
  • Circumference: The boundary of a circle.

2. Angle at the Centre and Circumference

Diagram 2: Angle at the Centre

The angle subtended by the same chord at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

Angle at the centre theorem

Angle at the centre = 2 × angle at the circumference, standing on the same arc.

3. Angles in the Same Segment

Angles subtended by the same chord at the circumference, in the same segment, are equal.

Angles in the same segment are equal.

4. Angle in a Semicircle

The angle subtended by a diameter at the circumference is always 90°.

Remember:

Angle in a semicircle = 90°.

5. Perpendicular from the Centre to a Chord

A perpendicular line drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.

Perpendicular from centre to chord → chord is bisected.

Unit 2: Cyclic Quadrilaterals

1. What is a Cyclic Quadrilateral?

A cyclic quadrilateral is a four-sided shape whose four vertices all lie on the circumference of the same circle.

Diagram 3: Cyclic Quadrilateral

All four vertices A, B, C and D lie on the circle.

2. Opposite Angles of a Cyclic Quadrilateral

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.

A + C = 180°

B + D = 180°

3. Exterior Angle of a Cyclic Quadrilateral

The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is equal to the opposite interior angle.

Exterior angle = Opposite interior angle

4. Converse of the Cyclic Quadrilateral Theorem

If a quadrilateral has a pair of opposite angles that add up to 180°, then the quadrilateral is cyclic.

Remember:

Opposite angles supplementary → quadrilateral is cyclic.

Unit 3: Tangents to a Circle

1. What is a Tangent?

A tangent is a straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point.

Diagram 4: Tangent to a Circle

The radius OT is perpendicular to the tangent at T.

2. Tangent and Radius Theorem

A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact.

Radius ⟂ Tangent

Angle between them = 90°

3. Equal Tangents

Tangents drawn from the same external point to a circle are equal in length.

Diagram 5: Equal Tangents

PA = PB because tangents drawn from the same external point are equal.

Equal tangents theorem:

PA = PB

4. Important Steps in Geometry Proofs

  1. Identify the given information.
  2. Look for radii, chords and tangents.
  3. Identify the relevant theorem.
  4. Use the theorem to calculate unknown angles or lengths.
  5. Give a reason for every statement.
