1. What is Trigonometry?

Trigonometry is the study of the relationship between angles and sides of triangles.

2. The Three Trigonometric Ratios

For a right-angled triangle:

SOH: sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse

CAH: cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse

TOA: tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Remember: SOH-CAH-TOA helps you remember the three trigonometric ratios.

3. Identifying the Sides

  • Hypotenuse: The longest side, opposite the right angle.
  • Opposite: The side opposite the angle θ.
  • Adjacent: The side next to θ, excluding the hypotenuse.

4. Special Angles

Learn these important values:

sin 30° = 1/2

cos 30° = √3/2

tan 30° = √3/3

sin 45° = √2/2

cos 45° = √2/2

tan 45° = 1

sin 60° = √3/2

cos 60° = 1/2

tan 60° = √3

5. The CAST Rule

The CAST rule helps determine which trigonometric ratios are positive in each quadrant.

  • Quadrant I: All ratios are positive.
  • Quadrant II: Sine is positive.
  • Quadrant III: Tangent is positive.
  • Quadrant IV: Cosine is positive.

6. The Basic Trigonometric Identity

sin² θ + cos² θ = 1

7. Important Reminders

  • Check whether your calculator is in degree mode when working with degrees.
  • Always identify the correct sides before choosing a ratio.
  • Show all working in calculations.
  • Use exact values for special angles where required.

Part 1: Simplifying Trigonometric Expressions

Example 1

cos²θ × tanθ + tanθ × sin²θ

Simplify the expression.

Step 1: Start with the expression.

cos²θ tanθ + tanθ sin²θ

Step 2: Factor out tanθ.

tanθ(cos²θ + sin²θ)

Step 3: Use the identity:

cos²θ + sin²θ = 1

Final Answer:

tanθ

Example 2

(2 tanθ cosθ sinθ) / (cos²θ − 1)

Step 1: Start with the expression.

2 tanθ cosθ sinθ / (cos²θ − 1)

Step 2: Use the identity:

cos²θ − 1 = −sin²θ

Step 3: Replace tanθ with sinθ/cosθ.

[2 × (sinθ/cosθ) × cosθ × sinθ] / (−sin²θ)

Step 4: Cancel common factors.

−2

Part 2: Proving Trigonometric Identities

An identity is an equation that is true for every allowed value of the variable.

When proving identities, simplify one side or both sides until they are equal.

Method 1: Simplify the Left-Hand Side

Prove that:
tanθ + cosθ/(1 + sinθ) = 1/cosθ

Step 1: Start with the LHS.

tanθ + cosθ/(1 + sinθ)

Step 2: Use tanθ = sinθ/cosθ.

sinθ/cosθ + cosθ/(1 + sinθ)

Step 3: Find a common denominator.

[sinθ(1 + sinθ) + cos²θ] / [cosθ(1 + sinθ)]

Step 4: Expand the numerator.

[sinθ + sin²θ + cos²θ] / [cosθ(1 + sinθ)]

Step 5: Use sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

(sinθ + 1) / [cosθ(1 + sinθ)]

Step 6: Cancel the common factor.

LHS = 1/cosθ = RHS
Identity proved.

Restrictions: cosθ ≠ 0 and 1 + sinθ ≠ 0.

Method 2: Simplify Both Sides

Prove that:
(sin²θ − cos²θ)/(sinθ cosθ + cos²θ) = tanθ − 1

Left-hand side:

Factorise the numerator and denominator:

[(sinθ − cosθ)(sinθ + cosθ)] / [cosθ(sinθ + cosθ)]

Cancel the common factor.

(sinθ − cosθ)/cosθ

= sinθ/cosθ − 1

= tanθ − 1

Right-hand side:

tanθ − 1

LHS = RHS
Identity proved.

Restrictions: cosθ ≠ 0 and sinθ + cosθ ≠ 0.

Part 3: Restrictions on Trigonometric Identities

Example 3

Prove that:
tan²θ × cos²θ = sin²θ

Step 1: Start with the LHS.

tan²θ × cos²θ

Step 2: Replace tan²θ.

(sin²θ/cos²θ) × cos²θ

Step 3: Cancel cos²θ.

sin²θ = RHS
Identity proved.

Restriction: cosθ ≠ 0.
