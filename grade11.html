Exponents and Surds

Learn the laws of exponents, rational exponents and how to simplify surds.

Part 1: Laws of Exponents

Exponents tell us how many times a number is multiplied by itself.

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

Important Laws of Exponents

1. Multiplication: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ

2. Division: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ

3. Power of a power: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ

4. Zero exponent: a⁰ = 1

5. Negative exponent: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ

6. Fractional exponent: a¹/ⁿ = ⁿ√a

Worked Example 1

Simplify:

2³ × 2²

Step 1: Identify the bases.

Both terms have base 2.

Step 2: Apply the multiplication law.

When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents.

2³ × 2² = 2³⁺²

Step 3: Add the exponents.

2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2

Final Answer: 32

Worked Example 2: Negative Exponents

Simplify:

2⁻³

Step 1: Recall the negative exponent law.

A negative exponent means that we take the reciprocal of the number.

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ

Step 2: Apply the law.

2⁻³ = 1/2³

Step 3: Calculate 2³.

2 × 2 × 2 = 8

Final Answer: 1/8

Part 2: Surds

A surd is an irrational number that contains a root and cannot be simplified to remove the root completely.

√2, √3, √5

1. Simplifying Surds

To simplify a surd, look for a perfect square that is a factor of the number inside the root.

Worked Example 3

Simplify:

√72

Step 1: Find a perfect square factor of 72.

72 = 36 × 2

Step 2: Separate the roots.

√72 = √36 × √2

Step 3: Simplify √36.

√36 = 6

Therefore:

√72 = 6√2

Final Answer: 6√2

2. Adding and Subtracting Surds

We can only add or subtract like surds. Like surds have the same number inside the root.

Worked Example 4

Simplify:

3√2 + 5√2 - 2√2

Step 1: Check the surds.

All three terms contain √2.

Step 2: Combine the coefficients.

3 + 5 - 2 = 6

Step 3: Keep the surd.

Final Answer: 6√2

3. Multiplying Surds

When multiplying square roots, multiply the numbers inside the roots.

√a × √b = √(ab)

Worked Example 5

Simplify:

√3 × √12

Step 1: Multiply the numbers inside the roots.

√3 × √12 = √36

Step 2: Calculate the square root.

√36 = 6

Final Answer: 6

4. Dividing Surds

√a / √b = √(a/b)

Worked Example 6

Simplify:

√50 / √2

Step 1: Combine the roots.

√50 / √2 = √(50/2)

Step 2: Divide.

√25

Step 3: Simplify.

Final Answer: 5

5. Rationalising the Denominator

Rationalising means removing a surd from the denominator of a fraction.

Worked Example 7

Simplify:

1 / √3

Step 1: Multiply the numerator and denominator by √3.

(1/√3) × (√3/√3)

Step 2: Multiply the numerator.

1 × √3 = √3

Step 3: Multiply the denominator.

√3 × √3 = 3

Final Answer: √3/3

Worked Example

Solve for x:

2x + 4 = 12

Step 1: Subtract 4 from both sides.

2x = 12 - 4

2x = 8

Functions and Graphs

Understand functions, how to read graphs, transformations and trigonometric graphs.

What is a Function?

A function is a relationship between an input value (x) and an output value (y).

Each input value must have exactly one output value.

y = f(x)

Part 1: Linear Functions

A linear function produces a straight-line graph. Its general form is:

y = mx + c

m: Gradient of the line.

c: y-intercept.

Example: y = 2x + 1

We can calculate some points:

When x = -1, y = -1

When x = 0, y = 1

When x = 1, y = 3

These points form a straight line.

Worked Example 1: Calculate the Gradient

Find the gradient between the points (1; 3) and (3; 7).

Step 1: Write down the gradient formula.

m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)

Step 2: Identify the coordinates.

(x₁; y₁) = (1; 3)

(x₂; y₂) = (3; 7)

Step 3: Substitute into the formula.

m = (7 - 3) / (3 - 1)

Step 4: Simplify.

m = 4 / 2

Final Answer: m = 2

Part 2: Quadratic Functions

A quadratic function produces a parabola. Its general form is:

y = ax² + bx + c

If a is positive, the parabola opens upwards.

If a is negative, the parabola opens downwards.

Example: y = x²

Calculate the following points:

When x = -2, y = 4

When x = -1, y = 1

When x = 0, y = 0

When x = 1, y = 1

When x = 2, y = 4

Worked Example 2: Calculate the Value of y

Given:

y = x² + 2

Calculate y when x = 3.

Step 1: Write down the function.

y = x² + 2

Step 2: Substitute x = 3.

y = 3² + 2

Step 3: Calculate the square.

y = 9 + 2

Final Answer: y = 11

Part 3: Hyperbolic Functions

A hyperbola is a graph with two separate branches. One common form is:

y = a/x

For this function, x cannot equal zero because division by zero is undefined.

Example: y = 4/x

When x = 1, y = 4

When x = 2, y = 2

When x = 4, y = 1

When x = -1, y = -4

Part 4: The Effect of Parameter p

Changing the value of a parameter can move a graph or change its appearance.

For example, consider:

y = x² + p

The parameter p moves the parabola up or down.

  • When p is positive, the graph moves upwards.
  • When p is negative, the graph moves downwards.
  • When p = 0, the graph is y = x².

Part 5: Average Gradient

The average gradient measures the change in y divided by the change in x between two points.

Average gradient = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)

Worked Example 3

Find the average gradient between (2; 4) and (4; 16).

Step 1: Write the formula.

Average gradient = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)

Step 2: Substitute the coordinates.

= (16 - 4) / (4 - 2)

Step 3: Simplify.

= 12 / 2

Final Answer: 6

Part 6: Trigonometric Graphs

Trigonometric graphs show how sine, cosine and tangent change as the angle changes.

The Basic Sine Graph

y = sin x

Important points on the sine graph:

sin 0° = 0

sin 90° = 1

sin 180° = 0

sin 270° = -1

sin 360° = 0

Your Trigonometric Graphs Study Image

Study how changing a and q affects the graph y = a sin x + q.

Remember:

  • Changing a affects the amplitude.
  • Changing q shifts the graph upwards or downwards.
  • The period of the basic sine graph is 360°.

Worked Example 4

Given:

y = 2 sin x

Determine the amplitude.

Step 1: Identify the general form.

y = a sin x

Step 2: Identify a.

a = 2

Step 3: The amplitude is the absolute value of a.

Amplitude = |2|

Final Answer: 2

Trigonometry

Understand sine, cosine, tangent, identities and trigonometric equations.

Basic Trigonometric Ratios

Part 1: The Fundamental Trigonometric Identities

There are two important identities that we use when simplifying and proving trigonometric expressions.

1. Quotient Identity

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ

The quotient identity tells us that tangent is equal to sine divided by cosine.

Restriction: This identity is defined only when cos θ ≠ 0.

Proof of the Quotient Identity

From the definitions of the trigonometric ratios:

sin θ = y/r
cos θ = x/r

Step 1: Start with the left-hand side.

sin θ / cos θ

Step 2: Substitute the ratios.

(y/r) / (x/r)

Step 3: Simplify the fraction.

(y/r) × (r/x) = y/x

Step 4: Recall the tangent ratio.

y/x = tan θ

Therefore, sin θ / cos θ = tan θ.

2. Square Identity (Pythagorean Identity)

sin² θ + cos² θ = 1

This identity is also called the Pythagorean identity. It is based on the theorem of Pythagoras.

Proof of the Square Identity

Step 1: Start with the left-hand side.

sin² θ + cos² θ

Step 2: Substitute the trigonometric ratios.

(y/r)² + (x/r)²

Step 3: Square each fraction.

y²/r² + x²/r²

Step 4: Combine the fractions.

(y² + x²) / r²

Step 5: Apply Pythagoras.

According to the theorem of Pythagoras:

x² + y² = r²

Therefore:

r² / r² = 1

Therefore, sin² θ + cos² θ = 1.

Alternative Forms of the Square Identity

We can rearrange the square identity to get four useful forms:

sin² θ = 1 - cos² θ

cos² θ = 1 - sin² θ

cos² θ - 1 = -sin² θ

sin² θ - 1 = -cos² θ

Part 2: Simplifying Trigonometric Expressions

To simplify trigonometric expressions, we use the quotient identity, square identity and basic algebra.

Worked Example 1

Simplify:

cos² θ · tan θ + tan θ · sin² θ

Step 1: Take out the common factor tan θ.

= tan θ (cos² θ + sin² θ)

Step 2: Apply the square identity.

cos² θ + sin² θ = 1

Therefore:

= tan θ (1)

Final Answer: tan θ

Worked Example 2

Simplify:

(2 tan θ · cos θ · sin θ) / (cos² θ - 1)

Step 1: Use the alternative form of the square identity.

cos² θ - 1 = -sin² θ

Substitute:

= (2 tan θ · cos θ · sin θ) / (-sin² θ)

Step 2: Cancel one factor of sin θ.

= (-2 tan θ · cos θ) / sin θ

Step 3: Use the quotient identity.

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ

= [-2 (sin θ / cos θ) · cos θ] / sin θ

Step 4: Simplify.

= -2 sin θ / sin θ

Final Answer: -2

Part 3: Proving Trigonometric Identities

To prove an identity, we must show that the left-hand side (LHS) is equal to the right-hand side (RHS).

We use the fundamental identities and basic algebra to simplify expressions.

Method 1: Simplify One Side Until It Equals the Other

Prove that:

tan θ + cos θ / (1 + sin θ) = 1 / cos θ

Step 1: Start with the LHS.

LHS = tan θ + cos θ / (1 + sin θ)

Step 2: Replace tan θ using the quotient identity.

= sin θ / cos θ + cos θ / (1 + sin θ)

Step 3: Find a common denominator.

= [sin θ(1 + sin θ) + cos² θ] / [cos θ(1 + sin θ)]

Step 4: Expand the numerator.

= [sin θ + sin² θ + cos² θ] / [cos θ(1 + sin θ)]

Step 5: Apply the square identity.

sin² θ + cos² θ = 1

= [sin θ + 1] / [cos θ(1 + sin θ)]

Step 6: Cancel the common factor.

= 1 / cos θ

LHS = RHS. Identity proved.

Restrictions: cos θ ≠ 0 and 1 + sin θ ≠ 0.

Method 2: Simplify Both Sides Until They Are Equal

Prove that:

(sin² θ - cos² θ) / (sin θ cos θ + cos² θ) = tan θ - 1

Step 1: Start with the LHS.

LHS = (sin² θ - cos² θ) / (sin θ cos θ + cos² θ)

Step 2: Factorise the numerator and denominator.

= [(sin θ - cos θ)(sin θ + cos θ)] / [cos θ(sin θ + cos θ)]

Step 3: Cancel the common factor.

= (sin θ - cos θ) / cos θ

Step 4: Now simplify the RHS.

RHS = tan θ - 1

Step 5: Apply the quotient identity.

= sin θ / cos θ - 1

Step 6: Use a common denominator.

= (sin θ - cos θ) / cos θ

LHS = RHS. Identity proved.

Restrictions: cos θ ≠ 0 and sin θ + cos θ ≠ 0.

Part 4: Restrictions on Trigonometric Identities

Trigonometric identities are not always defined for every value of θ.

We must check for values that make a denominator zero.

Identities Containing Tangent

Remember:

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ

Tangent is undefined whenever cos θ = 0.

In the interval 0° to 360°, this happens at:

θ = 90° or θ = 270°

Worked Example 3

Prove that:

tan² θ · cos² θ = sin² θ

Step 1: Start with the LHS.

LHS = tan² θ · cos² θ

Step 2: Replace tangent using the quotient identity.

= (sin² θ / cos² θ) · cos² θ

Step 3: Cancel cos² θ.

= sin² θ

LHS = RHS. Identity proved.

Restriction: cos θ ≠ 0, so θ cannot be 90° or 270° in the interval 0° to 360°.

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite / adjacent

Worked Example

Calculate sin(30°).

sin(30°) = ?

Step 1: Recall the special-angle value.

Step 2: Use the standard trigonometric value.

sin(30°) = 1/2

Final Answer: 1/2

Probability

Learn how to calculate the probability of events and solve probability problems.

Probability Formula

P(A) = Favourable outcomes / Total outcomes

Worked Example

A fair die has six sides. What is the probability of rolling a 4?

P(rolling a 4) = ?

Step 1: Count the possible outcomes.

There are 6 possible outcomes.

Step 2: Count the favourable outcomes.

Only one outcome is a 4.

Step 3: Apply the probability formula.

P(A) = Favourable outcomes / Total outcomes

P(4) = 1/6

Final Answer: 1/6
