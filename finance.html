Finance, Growth and Decay

Learn how money, investments and other quantities increase or decrease over time using compound growth and decay.

Unit 1: Compound Growth

1. What is Compound Growth?

Compound growth happens when a quantity increases by the same percentage over regular periods. Each increase is calculated using the new amount, not only the original amount.

Compound Growth Formula

A = P(1 + i)n

Where:

  • A = final amount
  • P = original amount (principal)
  • i = growth rate per period as a decimal
  • n = number of periods

2. Converting Percentages to Decimals

Before substituting the interest or growth rate into the formula, convert the percentage into a decimal.

5% = 5 ÷ 100 = 0.05

8% = 8 ÷ 100 = 0.08

12% = 12 ÷ 100 = 0.12

3. Compound Interest

Compound interest is interest calculated on the original amount and on interest already earned.

For example, if R1 000 earns 10% interest per year, the amount increases each year.

4. Example of Compound Growth

An investment of R1 000 earns compound interest at 10% per year for 3 years.

Year 1:

R1 000 × 1.10 = R1 100

Year 2:

R1 100 × 1.10 = R1 210

Year 3:

R1 210 × 1.10 = R1 331

Final amount = R1 331

5. Important Things to Remember

  • Always convert percentages into decimals.
  • Check whether the rate is annual or monthly.
  • Make sure the time and rate use the same period.
  • Compound growth uses a plus sign.
  • Do not round too early during calculations.
Remember: Compound growth means the amount increases over time because growth is calculated on the updated amount.

Unit 2: Decay

1. What is Decay?

Decay happens when a quantity decreases by the same percentage over regular periods.

Examples include the depreciation of cars, the decrease in the value of equipment and populations that decrease over time.

Compound Decay Formula

A = P(1 − i)n

Where:

  • A = final amount
  • P = original amount
  • i = decay rate as a decimal
  • n = number of periods

2. Depreciation

Depreciation is the decrease in the value of an asset over time.

For example, a car loses part of its value every year.

3. Example of Decay

A car is bought for R200 000. It depreciates by 10% per year. Calculate its value after 2 years.

Year 1:

R200 000 × 0.90 = R180 000

Year 2:

R180 000 × 0.90 = R162 000

Value after 2 years = R162 000

4. Understanding the Decay Factor

100% − 10% = 90%

90% ÷ 100 = 0.90

Therefore, the decay factor is 0.90.

5. Important Things to Remember

  • Decay means the amount decreases.
  • Convert the percentage into a decimal.
  • Subtract the rate from 1.
  • Use the number of periods as the exponent.
  • Check whether the question asks for the amount lost or the amount remaining.
Remember: Compound decay uses a minus sign because the quantity decreases over time.
