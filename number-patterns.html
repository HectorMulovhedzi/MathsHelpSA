Number Patterns with a Constant Second Difference

In this topic, we will learn how to identify quadratic number patterns using first differences and second differences. We will also learn how to determine the general term of a number pattern.

1. What is a Number Pattern?

A number pattern is a sequence of numbers arranged according to a particular rule.

Example:

3 ; 8 ; 15 ; 24 ; 35 ; ...

2. First Differences

The first difference is found by subtracting each term from the term immediately after it.

Example:

8 − 3 = 5

15 − 8 = 7

24 − 15 = 9

35 − 24 = 11

First differences: 5 ; 7 ; 9 ; 11

3. Second Differences

The second difference is found by calculating the differences between consecutive first differences.

Example:

7 − 5 = 2

9 − 7 = 2

11 − 9 = 2

Second differences: 2 ; 2 ; 2

Remember: If the second differences are constant, the number pattern is quadratic.

4. The General Term

A quadratic number pattern has a general term of the form:

Tn = an² + bn + c

Where:

  • Tn is the general term.
  • n is the term position.
  • a, b and c are constants.

5. Finding the Value of a

The constant second difference is equal to twice the value of a.

2a = Constant second difference

Therefore:

a = Constant second difference ÷ 2
Example:

Second difference = 2

2a = 2

a = 1

6. Finding the General Term

To determine the general term:

  1. Calculate the first differences.
  2. Calculate the second differences.
  3. Use the second difference to find a.
  4. Substitute a into Tn = an² + bn + c.
  5. Use the number pattern to find b and c.
  6. Write the final general term.

7. Important Formula

Tn = an² + bn + c

Constant second difference = 2a
