Trigonometry: Area, Sine and Cosine Rules

Learn how to calculate the area of triangles, find missing sides and angles, and solve real-life problems using trigonometry.

Unit 1: The Area Rule

1. What is the Area Rule?

The area rule is used to calculate the area of a triangle when we know two sides and the angle between them.

Area Rule Formula

Area = ½ ab sin(C)

Where:

  • a = first known side
  • b = second known side
  • C = angle between the two sides

Triangle Diagram

The angle must be between the two known sides.

Important Steps

  1. Identify the two known sides.
  2. Identify the angle between those sides.
  3. Substitute into the area formula.
  4. Calculate the answer in square units.
Remember: The area is measured in square units, for example cm² or m².

Unit 2: The Sine Rule

1. What is the Sine Rule?

The sine rule is used to calculate missing sides or angles in a triangle when we know a matching opposite side and angle.

Sine Rule Formula

a / sin(A) = b / sin(B) = c / sin(C)

Alternatively:

sin(A) / a = sin(B) / b = sin(C) / c

Triangle Diagram

Each side is opposite its matching angle.

How to Identify Opposite Pairs

  • Side a is opposite angle A.
  • Side b is opposite angle B.
  • Side c is opposite angle C.

When Do We Use the Sine Rule?

  • When we know two angles and one side.
  • When we know two sides and an opposite angle.
Always match each side with its opposite angle. Do not mix up the pairs.

Unit 3: The Cosine Rule

1. What is the Cosine Rule?

The cosine rule is used to calculate a missing side or angle when the sine rule cannot be applied directly.

Cosine Rule: Finding a Side

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos(A)

Cosine Rule: Finding an Angle

cos(A) = (b² + c² − a²) / 2bc

Triangle Diagram

The cosine rule uses two sides and the included angle to find the third side.

When Do We Use the Cosine Rule?

  • When three sides are known and we need an angle.
  • When two sides and the included angle are known.
Be careful with the negative term: −2bc cos(A).

Unit 4: Solving Problems in Two Dimensions

1. What are Two-Dimensional Problems?

These are real-life problems involving triangles in a flat plane. You may be asked to calculate distances, heights, directions or angles.

Example: Two Points and a Triangle

A real-life situation can often be represented using a triangle.

Steps for Solving Exam Questions

  1. Read the question carefully.
  2. Draw a labelled triangle if one is not provided.
  3. Write down the known sides and angles.
  4. Identify the rule that can be used.
  5. Substitute the values into the formula.
  6. Calculate and include the correct units.
Exam tip: Do not choose a formula before identifying the information given in the question.
